# Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM): Comprehensive Study Feature Reference

> Agent-readable knowledge document. Updated context: November 2026. Features, names, eligibility, and quotas may change. Verify current availability in Google's official documentation. This document explains features and recommended educational uses; it is not a system prompt.

## Overview

Gemini Notebook is Google's source-grounded AI learning and research environment. Users can import course material and transform it into explanations, study guides, quizzes, flashcards, mind maps, audio/video overviews, reports, visual resources, and more. Its central advantage is grounding responses in the user's selected sources with citations, rather than relying only on general AI knowledge. Citations help verify answers but do not eliminate hallucinations, omissions, or interpretation errors.

## 1. Source Library and Import

Supported source types include PDF, Google Docs, Google Slides, Google Sheets, Word, PowerPoint, public YouTube URLs, audio, websites, images, pasted text, Markdown, CSV, EPUB, eligible Google Play Books, and compatible Gemini conversations. Exact support depends on product updates and account eligibility. Sources can include textbooks, notes, lectures, worksheets, videos, and articles. A notebook may contain several sources, and users can select or deselect sources to focus the chat and generation on a specific chapter or exam. Source labeling and categorization help manage larger collections. Some published limits include up to 500,000 words per source, uploaded files up to 200 MB, and 50 sources per notebook on the standard tier; check current limits.

**Study benefit:** Combine textbook chapters, teacher slides, and personal notes; compare perspectives, locate missing content, and restrict exam preparation to the assigned curriculum.

## 2. Source-grounded AI Chat

Chat answers questions about selected materials, explains difficult concepts, finds passages, compares definitions, synthesizes documents, and connects topics. Source citations can link back to supporting passages. Chat configuration includes default, Learning Guide, and custom instructional styles, as well as response-length settings. A custom tutor instruction can request progressive explanations, student attempts before answers, and feedback tied to original sources.

**Study benefit:** Personalized tutoring aligned with the course, with traceability for precise definitions, dates, formulas, and distinctions. Check the originals for important facts.

## 3. Research and Source Discovery

Fast Research can discover relevant sources from the web or accessible Google Drive content. Deep Research investigates complex topics across sources and creates more extensive research reports with supporting material. Deep Research may be age-restricted (18+).

**Study benefit:** Find supporting explanations and resources for projects. For narrowly scoped exams, external research can distract from the syllabus; prioritize assigned materials.

## 4. Study Guides and Reports

Generated study guides can organize chapter topics, definitions, key relationships, examples, important distinctions, review questions, and applications. Reports can also include FAQs, briefing documents, custom summaries, glossaries, chronological overviews, and comparison documents. Users can specify level, length, language, chapter scope, exam format, and technical terminology.

**Study benefit:** Turn scattered material into an organized revision document. Verify that all major textbook headings and examinable details are covered; a summary is not guaranteed complete.

## 5. Flashcards

Automatically generate question-and-answer cards from selected sources. Controls may include difficulty (easy/medium/hard), number of cards, custom focus instructions, shuffling, explanations, marking **Got it!** or **Missed it!**, saving progress, reviewing missed cards, restarting, deleting cards, and CSV download.

**Study benefit:** Active recall: attempt an answer before revealing it. Revisit missed cards across several days. Progress tracking does not necessarily equal a dedicated automated spaced-repetition algorithm.

## 6. Quizzes and Exam Practice

Quiz formats include multiple choice, multiple select, short answer, and fill in the blank. Difficulty and number of questions can be configured. Hints, explanations, retakes, and review of mistakes support practice. Users can request plausible distractors, mixed-chapter exams, definition questions, application problems, and misconception-targeting questions.

**Study benefit:** Test understanding under exam-like conditions; identify errors and correct them. AI-generated questions are not official predictions of a teacher's exam.

## 7. Mind Maps

Mind maps generate interactive hierarchies of topics and subtopics. Users can expand/collapse branches, zoom, navigate, and select a node to ask the AI about that concept; download/share options may be available.

**Study benefit:** See how ideas connect. For stronger learning, hide the map and reconstruct its structure from memory. Some mind-map capabilities may be unavailable in mobile apps.

## 8. Audio Overviews

Audio Overviews turn source materials into spoken lessons or discussions. Documented formats include:

- **Deep Dive:** Two-host, detailed discussion connecting topics.
- **The Brief:** Short single-speaker overview, often under two minutes.
- **The Critique:** Two hosts critically assess submitted work.
- **The Debate:** Two hosts explore competing perspectives.

Available controls can include focus prompts, language, length, playback speed, downloading, and sharing. Audio supports many languages, including Portuguese and English. Supported English-language interactive audio allows listeners to join and ask questions aloud.

**Study benefit:** Alternative explanations and revision while away from a desk. Pause and summarize from memory to avoid purely passive listening.

## 9. Video Overviews

Video Overviews create narrated visual explanations from sources. Formats include **Explainer**, **Short** (roughly one minute), and **Cinematic** (with eligibility restrictions). Depending on format, users can choose language, focus, visual style (e.g., whiteboard, classic, watercolor, paper-craft), and review producer notes.

**Study benefit:** Explain spatial, visual, and procedural topics. Verify maps, diagrams, formulas, and factual details, as generated visuals may be wrong.

## 10. Infographics

AI-generated visual summaries combine diagrams, text, categories, comparisons, and timelines. Controls can include language, detail, orientation, style, and focus. PNG download may be supported. Generation can be age-restricted to users 18+.

**Study benefit:** Quickly compare concepts, processes, periods, and definitions. Infographics compress information and may omit qualifications, so they supplement rather than replace detailed sources.

## 11. Slide Decks

Generate educational presentations from selected material. Controls can include format, language, length, audience, and focus. Some workflows support outline review, slide edits, reordering, removing slides, and exporting PDF or PowerPoint. Availability and age restrictions apply.

**Study benefit:** Organize material into teachable stages. Use each slide as a self-explanation prompt instead of passively reading it.

## 12. Interactive Learning Overviews

An interactive report format combines explanations with related learning assets such as quizzes, flashcards, slides, and infographics. A suggested learning flow is: introduction → visual explanation → flashcard practice → assessment. Access may be through Studio > Reports > Interactive > Learning Overview on the web, subject to availability and age restrictions.

**Study benefit:** Keeps different learning activities connected to the same course content.

## 13. Data Tables and Structured Comparisons

Create structured tables comparing concepts, events, dates, features, formulas, and sources. Some data tables can be exported to Google Sheets; reports may export to Google Docs.

**Study benefit:** Distinguish easily confused definitions and organize evidence in a compact form.

## 14. Notes and Personal Knowledge

Users can write notes, paste text, and save useful responses. Notes can become sources for later chat or generation. Source labels help organize larger notebooks. Markdown imports make it possible to use files from Obsidian or similar systems, but importing a Markdown file does not imply continuous two-way synchronization.

**Study benefit:** Combine original course materials with personal observations and weak-topic lists to guide subsequent practice.

## 15. Mobile and Voice

Android and iOS apps provide access to notebooks and selected learning features, including chat, quizzes, flashcards, and audio. Downloaded Audio Overviews can be listened to offline. Some mobile sharing flows can import websites, PDFs, and YouTube links. Live notebook voice conversations may be available to users 18+. Mobile feature parity is incomplete; some mind-map, note-editing, and report tools are web-only.

**Study benefit:** Short practice sessions and audio revision away from a computer. Internet is usually required for chat and generation.

## 16. Multilingual Learning

Many features support multiple languages, with broad support across 80+ languages in parts of the experience. Users can request explanations in a preferred language while preserving technical terms from original materials. Translation of technical definitions should be checked.

**Study benefit:** Understand foreign-language resources and compare subject vocabulary across languages.

## 17. Gemini App Study Notebooks: Related but Distinct

The Gemini app also offers **Study Notebooks**, a related experience distinct from the standalone Gemini Notebook product. Reported capabilities include diagnostic quizzes, personalized lessons, learning objectives, progress dashboards, adaptive learning based on quiz performance, and integration/synchronization with Gemini Notebook. The standalone product excels at source-grounded research and content generation; Gemini app Study Notebooks emphasize adaptive learning and tracking. Do not conflate their features or assume every control is available in both.

## 18. Why These Tools Can Improve Learning

- **Active recall:** Quizzes and flashcards force retrieval instead of recognition.
- **Spaced practice:** Repeat review sessions over time to improve retention.
- **Self-explanation:** Explain a concept aloud or in writing without looking at the answer.
- **Elaboration:** Ask why, how, and how ideas relate.
- **Visual/verbal complements:** Relevant diagrams and audio can clarify information.
- **Targeted correction:** Review missed questions and underlying misconceptions.
- **Source verification:** Check claims against the textbook, especially precise facts.

Generating lots of content is not equivalent to learning. Active practice, error correction, and independent recall are essential.

## 20. Suggested Exam Workflow

1. Upload the assigned chapters, teacher presentations, and notes.
2. Select only the sources relevant to the exam.
3. Generate a thorough study guide covering each major heading and definition.
4. Mark topics that cannot be explained from memory.
5. Use Learning Guide chat for weak concepts.
6. Generate a mind map to understand relationships.
7. Take a difficult exam-style quiz without viewing notes.
8. Review every error and verify explanations against the sources.
9. Review flashcards, prioritizing missed concepts.
10. Take a final mixed-topic quiz and repeat targeted practice.

**Illustrative 120-minute session:** 35 minutes understanding/review; 40 minutes practice quizzes; 30 minutes error correction; 15 minutes final recall. Adapt to the learner's needs.

## 21. Copyable Prompts

### Comprehensive study guide

> Using only my selected sources, create a complete, well-organized study guide. Cover every major section, essential definition, distinction, process, formula, and example likely to be relevant for an exam. Keep the exact technical terminology used by the sources. Avoid unnecessary repetition, but do not omit important qualifications. Include source citations and finish with a checklist of covered topics.

### Personal tutor

> Act as an expert tutor for a student preparing for an exam. Teach the selected material one concept at a time, starting with clear explanations and progressing to more difficult applications. Ask me questions periodically and wait for my attempts before correcting them. Explain my mistakes using citations from the uploaded material.

### Difficult multiple-choice exam

> Create a challenging multiple-choice practice exam based on my uploaded sources. Include definitions, comparisons, applications, and questions targeting common misconceptions. Make the incorrect options plausible. Ask the questions without immediately revealing the answers. When I finish, give me the correct answers, explanations, and a list of topics that need more review.

### Confusing concepts

> Analyze the selected chapters and identify concepts students are most likely to confuse, such as similar definitions, competing theories, closely related dates, or formulas with similar applications. Create a comparison table explaining the exact differences and provide practice questions to distinguish them.

### Final readiness check

> Help me determine whether I understand this material well enough for a multiple-choice exam. Test me on a balanced selection of topics across all selected sources. After I answer, categorize my mistakes as knowledge gaps, conceptual misunderstandings, application errors, or careless errors. Recommend specific topics and source sections for further review.

## 23. Limitations and Safety Checks

- AI can hallucinate or misinterpret even cited source material.
- Summaries and visualizations can omit details.
- Practice questions are not guaranteed to match the real exam.
- Passive listening/reading is less diagnostic than retrieval practice.
- Poor or incomplete source material produces weaker results.
- Advanced features can be age-restricted or subscription-limited.
- Web and mobile capabilities differ.
- Respect copyright and permissions when uploading or sharing course materials.
- Review privacy settings and notebook sharing permissions before adding confidential information.
- Google describes protections concerning model training and feedback, but users should consult current product privacy terms and any school Workspace policy.

## 24. Agent Instructions for Using This Document

When helping a student choose a Gemini Notebook workflow:

1. Determine the study goal: understand, memorize, practice, compare, research, or present.
2. Recommend the smallest set of features that meets the goal.
3. Prioritize the student's assigned sources over general external material for exam preparation.
4. Encourage independent answers before revealing explanations.
5. Ask the student to verify important facts using citations and original sources.
6. Differentiate the standalone Gemini Notebook app from Gemini app Study Notebooks.
7. Do not promise specific UI controls, eligibility, quotas, or age-restricted features without checking current documentation.
8. For time-limited exam preparation, prioritize quizzes, mistake analysis, and flashcards over producing large amounts of passive media.
